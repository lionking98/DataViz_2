Australian Television Genres

How have drama, documentary, children’s programs and reality TV changed in Australia?

Fewer local drama titles entered production in 2024–25. Drama still took the largest share of viewing on broadcaster apps among the four genres examined here.

Production Viewing Paid streaming Maps

What gets made?

Production changed in different directions across genres. Documentary hours more than doubled from 444 to 1,175 between the two ABS surveys, while children’s hours fell from 865 to 377. More recently, general TV drama titles entering production dropped from 44 to 32 in 2024–25, with subscription drama accounting for most of that fall.

What do people watch?

Drama was the strongest of these four genres on broadcaster apps such as ABC iview and 9Now. In the first half of 2026 it accounted for 20.7% of app viewing minutes, more than twice its 8.8% share on broadcast TV. Reality and children’s programs also claimed larger shares on apps, while documentary leaned slightly toward broadcast TV.

What is available on paid streaming?

Adult drama dominated the Australian catalogue reported by paid services such as Netflix, Stan and Prime Video. In 2024–25 it accounted for 3,545 hours, about 65% of the hours across the four genres shown. Adult drama spending also rose by A$108.1m, more than the A$72.4m net increase across all genres because spending elsewhere fell.

Where are shows made and available?

Queensland’s A$619m rise put it ahead of NSW for drama production spending in 2024–25, and Victoria gained A$388m. High-budget international titles accounted for most of Queensland’s total, showing how a few major productions can reshape the spending map. Overseas, North America had the deepest reported Australian streaming catalogue: an average of 455 hours per reporting country, about 1.7 times South America’s 266 hours.

What stands out

  • Fewer subscription dramas absorbed more money. Titles entering production fell from 28 to 18 in 2024–25, while spending rose from A$471m to A$492m. Average reported spending per title climbed from about A$16.8m to A$27.3m, a 62% increase.
  • Drama led these genres on broadcaster apps. It accounted for 20.7% of app viewing minutes in the first half of 2026, more than twice its 8.8% share on broadcast TV. Children’s programs (9.6% versus 4.4%) and reality (13.3% versus 7.4%) also had larger shares on apps; documentary was the exception.
  • Adult drama drove the paid-streaming spending rise. ACMA’s reported spending increased by A$72.4m overall, but adult drama alone rose A$108.1m. Falls in sport and documentary spending offset part of that gain, so the net increase was smaller than the drama increase.
  • A few big productions reshaped the spending map. High-budget international titles accounted for 85% of Queensland’s 2024–25 drama expenditure, while four titles made up 62% of Victoria’s. Screen Australia also identifies studio and crew resources and state incentives as factors affecting where productions land.
